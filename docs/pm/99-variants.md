## Variant A — Direct-to-Consumer, Performance-Curious

### Framing

A beautiful consumer product targeting **Cohort C** (the performance-curious recreational rider — Whoop/Oura crossover demographic) and **Cohort A** (the competitive amateur). The product positions itself as **"the recovery dashboard for your horse"** — a daily readiness score, training-load tracking, recovery curves, and the Anthropic-powered AI chat.

This is what the current demo is naturally optimized for. The visual design language, the conversational AI tone, and the emphasis on individual horse-by-horse engagement all point at this customer.

### Distribution

Direct-to-consumer via:
- Instagram and TikTok content (founder + 2 partner-rider influencers)
- Equine athletic-performance podcast sponsorships
- Equestrian competition booth presence at 4-6 major regional events
- Affiliate program with sport-rider coaches

CAC estimate: **€40-80** (paid acquisition + organic content blend).

### Primary risks

1. **Willingness-to-pay among Cohort C is unproven.** Whoop's success is precedent but human-athletic-performance ≠ pet-athletic-performance. The optimization-vs-loss-prevention stake distinction (Phase 3) suggests Cohort C may treat €25/month as discretionary, especially during economic tightening. **Cancellation rate could spike in any consumer-spending downturn.**

2. **Engagement-bait failure mode.** Cohort C has been trained by Whoop to expect daily metrics, streaks, and gamification. Carrot Watch's deliberate anti-gamification posture (P6.3 rejection) may feel underwhelming to them. **Risk:** product feels too quiet vs. the products they're comparing it to.

3. **Vet-credibility deficit.** Without vet endorsement, Cohort C's vet may dismiss the data, which undermines the product's perceived legitimacy for the customer's own use. **Risk:** customers stop trusting the product after one vet visit.

4. **Sensor cost passthrough.** At Cohort C's expected WTP ceiling (~€25/month), the sensor wholesale cost (~€20-50) leaves negative-to-thin margin. **Variant A only works at the high end of Cohort C — competition-level riders willing to pay €40+/month.**

### Kill metric differences (vs. Variant B)

| Bet | Variant A specific threshold |
|-----|------------------------------|
| Bet 1 (baseline) | Same |
| Bet 2 (alerts) | **Higher tolerance for false positives** — Cohort C is engagement-tolerant; threshold can be at score ≥0.75 |
| Bet 3 (morning summary) | Restructured as a **readiness score**, not a reassurance card |
| Bet 4 (vet PDF) | **Optional** in Variant A — Cohort C doesn't drive vet referrals |
| Bet 5 (concierge) | **Replaced** with influencer-led onboarding — sport-rider partner posts unboxing content |
| Bet 6 (vet channel) | **Replaced** with content/influencer channel — bet succeeds if ≥3 partner riders generate ≥30 signups within 60 days |

### Asymmetric advantage best leveraged
- **Personal response to every signup email** translates well to a community-driven brand
- **Reversing a major roadmap decision in one meeting** lets the team chase the actual usage patterns (which may diverge from the demo's design)

### When to choose Variant A
- Switching interviews show 3+ Cohort C buyers describing willingness-to-pay >€30/month
- Thread mining surfaces strong "HRV for horses" / "Whoop for horses" vocabulary in equestrian sport communities
- The founder has direct relationships with 2+ visible equestrian sport influencers willing to partner
- The founder personally identifies more strongly with Cohort C than Cohort D as the user

## Variant B — Concierge Service, Vet-Channel, Loss-Prevention

### Framing

A high-touch service targeting **Cohort D** (post-incident owners) and **Cohort B** (small yard managers / breeders). The product positions itself as **"the early-warning system your vet recommends"** — quietness, reassurance, vet-readability, and the morning overnight summary as the centerpiece.

This is the artifact's analytical recommendation. It does not match the demo's instinctive design but matches the surviving hypotheses from Phase 3 ACH.

### Distribution

Vet-channel + community-driven:
- 30-vet outreach (Bet 6) → ongoing partnerships
- Post-incident landing pages co-branded with veterinary practices
- Content marketing in colic / laminitis / Cushing's Facebook groups
- Equine insurance company partnerships (longer-term — month 6+)

CAC estimate: **€10-30 via vet referral, €30-50 via community content** (lower than Variant A because the buying intent is higher).

### Primary risks

1. **Vet adoption may not materialize.** Bet 6's 30-day kill metric (response rate <20%) is real. If vets don't bite, the entire distribution thesis collapses. **Mitigation:** Variant B's success or failure becomes clear within 8 weeks of outreach — fast enough to pivot.

2. **Concierge onboarding doesn't scale.** Founder time runs out at ~100-150 customers under this model. **Variant B is a service-business in disguise** until the patterns can be productized.

3. **Cohort D is episodic.** Recently-traumatized owners' urgency fades after 6-9 months. **Retention beyond month 9 requires expanding into Cohort B or Cohort A** — which means the product surface must broaden over time.

4. **Selling to yards is operationally heavy.** Cohort B's WTP is higher but the sales cycle is longer, the install is multi-horse, and the failure modes are public (a horse loss with the product installed is a brand catastrophe). **Variant B can lead with Cohort D and graduate to Cohort B once the alert quality is proven.**

### Kill metric differences (vs. Variant A)

| Bet | Variant B specific threshold |
|-----|------------------------------|
| Bet 1 (baseline) | Same |
| Bet 2 (alerts) | **Lower tolerance for false positives** — Cohort D is anxiety-driven; threshold at score ≥0.85 with 3-gate logic. Drop urgent push entirely if FP rate >15%. |
| Bet 3 (morning summary) | Centerpiece — "quiet night" framing |
| Bet 4 (vet PDF) | **Critical path** — Variant B fails without it |
| Bet 5 (concierge) | **Mandatory** for first 50 customers |
| Bet 6 (vet channel) | **The bet that determines viability** — 30-day kill at <20% vet response rate forces pivot |

### Asymmetric advantage best leveraged
- **Serving a yard size too small for enterprise sales** is exactly the Cohort B sweet spot
- **Running an experiment that would embarrass a big company** — Cohort D's vulnerability requires care no public company will give

### When to choose Variant B
- Switching interviews show 3+ buyers in 90-day post-incident window
- Thread mining surfaces high-engagement colic / illness threads with no product mentioned
- Vet outreach micro-test (10 emails) gets ≥4 responses within 7 days
- The founder is willing to commit to a service-business model for 6-12 months before automation

## Decision rubric

Score each variant after evidence-gathering. Higher score wins.

| Signal | Variant A points | Variant B points |
|--------|------------------|------------------|
| Of 5 switching interviews, ≥3 describe post-incident or yard-manager triggers | 0 | +3 |
| Of 5 switching interviews, ≥3 describe performance/optimization triggers | +3 | 0 |
| Vet email micro-test: ≥4 of 10 responses in 7 days | 0 | +3 |
| Thread mining surfaces ≥10 colic / illness threads, no product mentioned | 0 | +2 |
| Thread mining surfaces ≥5 "HRV for horses" / sport-recovery threads | +2 | 0 |
| Sensor wholesale cost confirmed ≤€30/month/horse | +1 (margin works) | +1 (concierge value is higher) |
| Sensor wholesale cost confirmed >€50/month/horse | -2 (margin doesn't work) | -1 (still works but tighter) |
| Founder has 2+ existing equestrian sport-influencer relationships | +3 | 0 |
| Founder has 2+ existing equine-vet relationships | 0 | +3 |
| Founder has €100k+ for 12-month runway | +1 (paid acquisition viable) | 0 (concierge time is cheaper) |
| Founder has <€30k for 12 months | -2 (paid acquisition too expensive) | +1 (concierge / vet outreach lean) |

If neither variant scores meaningfully higher (within 2 points), **the evidence isn't yet strong enough** — run 5 more switching interviews before choosing.

## What stays the same across both variants

- The morning summary (Bet 3) is the product identity in **both** variants — only the *content* differs (reassurance vs. readiness score)
- Multi-gate confidence-based alerts (Bet 2) — both
- Personalised baselines (Bet 1) — both
- No proprietary sensor hardware (AVOID-1) — both
- Postgres-only minimal architecture (Phase 8) — both
- Concierge onboarding for first 50 (Bet 5) — both, though Variant A's concierge is content-driven, Variant B's is founder-led
- Pull-based glanceable UI default (MUST-3) — both

The shared core means a decision at day 60 is recoverable. You can pivot from A to B by changing distribution and tone without rebuilding the product.

---

## A note on this artifact's bias

The artifact analytically prefers Variant B. This is honest analysis, **but it is partial information**. The artifact does not know whether the founder has existing sport-rider relationships, marketing capital for paid acquisition, or a personal narrative that fits Cohort C better than Cohort D.

**The founder is right to push back on the recommendation** if the surrounding context favors Variant A. But the push-back should be quantified via the decision rubric, not asserted on instinct. The whole artifact is designed to convert instinct into testable bets.
